## About me

I am a sociologist with a PhD in Learning Sciences and Digital Technologies, awarded in May 2026. My research lies at the intersection of environmental sociology, ecoliteracy, artificial intelligence literacy, civic education and digital cultures. I study how adolescents develop ecological, digital and civic competences, and how human agency is shaped within interconnected technological, social and environmental systems.

I received the national MED “Roberto Giannatelli” Award for doctoral research on Eco-Digital Posthumanism.

Laurea in Sociology, University of Salerno, 2004. Previous experience includes sociological work in nonprofit and public-sector contexts and university teaching in General Sociology and Sociology of Education.

## Research projects

### EcoAI Framework

An interdisciplinary theoretical and empirical framework integrating ecoliteracy and artificial intelligence literacy into civic education. Its four analytically distinct but interdependent domains are:

1. Systems Understanding
2. Ecological Understanding of AI
3. Identity Formation
4. Tool-Based Learning

[www.ecoaiframework.com](https://www.ecoaiframework.com)

### EcoAI Index

A psychometric instrument comprising five latent dimensions: Eco-Digital Information Literacy (INFO), AI Awareness and Critical Attitudes (AATI), Eco-Civic Values (VAL), Eco-Digital Civic Agency (AGY) and Eco-Posthuman Agency (EPAS).

The research programme comprises an exploratory study (N=403) and a confirmatory administration (N=198). The final CFA/SEM analytical sample is N=151; index construction and invariance analyses use N=191. Model fit: CFI=.978, TLI=.975, RMSEA=.064, SRMR=.082.

### Eco-Digital Posthumanism

A theoretical framework examining agency, identity and responsibility within eco-digital systems. It conceptualizes eco-digital co-responsible agency as relational and situated across human, technological and environmental components.

[Read the SocArXiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/u4mrn_v1)

### The Algorithmic Oracle

A research programme on delegated judgment under uncertainty. It investigates how large language models can acquire oracular authority through privately enacted and infrastructurally governed processes of interpretive delegation.
